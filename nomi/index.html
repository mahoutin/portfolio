01 / Context

The interface is
the conversation.

“Where did my paycheck go?” is a real question. A dashboard isn’t always the right answer.

Nomi started as WithBFF, a private-beta financial assistant. I designed the product experience and built its stack: React, Python, Supabase, Plaid, Twilio, and an LLM orchestration layer.

SMS makes the details matter. A confusing answer cannot hide behind a clean dashboard. I have to get the words, timing, and financial logic right together.

A budget should feel like something
you helped make.
02 / Decisions

Three changes
that shaped the product.

01

Earn agreement before setting a budget.

The first onboarding flow generated a budget immediately after bank connection. Beta feedback showed a mismatch: a budget could be technically valid without feeling like something the user had agreed to. I redesigned the experience around a seven-day progression, from connection to confirmation to collaborative budgeting.

The tradeoff More steps before a complete budget, in exchange for explicit participation in what the product tracks.

02

Let tools compose around the question.

A rigid intent classifier struggled with compound questions and everyday language. I moved toward LLM orchestration: the model selects financial tools, retrieves the relevant data, and composes a response around the user’s question.

The tradeoff More orchestration complexity and model cost. Tool selection and financial accuracy still need careful evaluation.

03

Separate spending from money movement.

A transfer to savings should not read like overspending. I separated spending, saving, debt reduction, income, and internal transfers so the product could describe what actually happened to the money.

The tradeoff A more detailed classification layer underneath a simpler explanation to the user.

03 / Interface

One small surface.
A lot of product thinking.

01

Answer the actual question

Bring relevant spending context into the reply instead of sending someone to a dashboard.

02

Make the reasoning legible

A useful answer explains which spending or budget information it is based on.

03

Leave room for correction

When a user disagrees, ask whether the issue is the amount, date, or category before retrying.

04 / Reflection

What the work
changed for me.

The useful feedback came after I thought the design was done.

Onboarding, tool orchestration, and transaction classification are connected design decisions. A clearer message depends on the system underneath it being able to support that promise.

The next work is making the experience more reliable over time: evaluating compound questions, exposing uncertainty in incomplete data, and testing whether the onboarding sequence earns understanding without creating unnecessary delay.

Nomi is an independently founded product in private beta. This case study focuses on the product decisions, rather than measured adoption outcomes.
