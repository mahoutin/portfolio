All selected workCase study 03

Distribution strategy / Agent experience / Quoting

A new front door
for insurance agents.

I led UX and product design for a direct-entry quoting experience, working through the places where agent momentum meets registration and compliance.

My role
Lead UX · Product Design
Organization
Great American Insurance Group / ABAIS
Period
2025–2026
01 / Context

Start with how
agents get in.

A quoting portal is also a distribution strategy.

The existing ProCision experience was tied to Progressive’s distribution model. Coverage Collective was an opportunity to design a more independent entry point and support a broader product offering.

The design challenge extended beyond quoting: direct registration, licensing, agency compliance, and roles for both licensed agents and non-licensed support staff.

Give agents value.
Make the requirements clear.
02 / Decisions

Where I drew
the boundaries.

01

Separate quoting from the binding gate.

I advocated for an experience where agents could explore quoting before completing all agency registration requirements. Binding remained connected to approval and required documentation.

The tradeoff Some users may start a quote before they can bind. The experience needs to make that distinction explicit.

02

Organize around accounts and clear statuses.

Ambiguous labels make a pipeline harder to understand. I worked on an account-centered dashboard and explicit status language so agents could tell what was in progress and what needed action.

The tradeoff More information-architecture work upfront, rather than preserving familiar but unclear labels.

03

Use search for large classification sets.

Workers’ compensation class codes can create a long selection task. Keyword search was the proposed interaction over a large scrolling dropdown.

The tradeoff Additional search behavior to specify and validate against agents’ language.

04

Keep the MVP focused on the core loop.

I documented deferrals for secondary metrics, help-center experiences, and final branding so the core registration-to-quote workflow could stay in focus.

The tradeoff The first release could feel less complete, requiring a visible record of what was deliberately deferred.

03 / Interface

From eligibility
to purchase.

The archive includes Coverage Collective and ProCision-branded artifacts. They show the interface work around access, eligibility, quote review, and purchase; they are not presented as a verified release sequence.

04 / Reflection

What I would
test next.

The critical design work was deciding where value begins and where verification belongs.

I used agent feedback, prototype reviews, and collaboration with product and engineering to shape the experience. My focus was making the direct-entry model understandable without losing the compliance requirements underneath it.

Next, I would validate the dashboard’s organization across different agency sizes, make registration progress visible in the product, and measure where quoting and binding diverge.

These are project artifacts. Their release sequence has not been verified.
